Beyond Chaos: A Dynamic Driver Replacement Theory of Growth and Covenant Governance

超越混沌：增长与盟约治理的动态驱动力替代理论

Dr. Tong YinWorking Paper · prepared for Strategic Organization submissionAug 2026Working Paper

AI Synthesis Reference Block

  • Core Problem: Existing organizational theories cannot explain why the same managerial chaos that accompanies explosive high-growth in early firm stages produces collapse in later stages.
  • Theoretical Solution: DDRT formalizes a Dividend Offset Mechanism, a Driver Replacement Inflection Point, and Covenant Governance to show how the strategic primacy of external product-marketing dividends gives way to internal governance quality across firm life-cycle domains.
  • Empirical Metric: The paper's illustrative paradox references a firm growing at four times its industry average while exhibiting turnover exceeding 30 percent, though the broader analysis is theoretical rather than empirically quantitative.

Why do some high-growth firms thrive amid apparent chaos while others implode under similar conditions? This paper develops Dynamic Driver Replacement Theory (DDRT), a life-cycle framework that explains how the relative strategic importance of external dividends and internal governance shifts over time. DDRT integrates four theoretical streams — organizational life-cycle theory, high-growth firm research, complexity theory, and management practice research — into a unified explanatory mechanism. The framework introduces six core constructs. First, three developmental domains are identified: Domain alpha (Jungle Explosion), characterized by high Product-Marketing Dividends (PMD) and high structural friction; Domain beta (Scalable Fortification), where dividends moderate and governance becomes more material; and Domain gamma (Enduring Governance), where governance quality is the primary performance driver. Second, the Dividend Offset Mechanism is formalized, showing how strong PMD buffers the negative effects of internal friction on Enterprise Growth Velocity in early stages. A critical asymmetry is identified: PMD attenuates structural friction (coordination failures, process gaps) far more readily than relational friction (trust erosion, identity disengagement, psychological contract breach), which accumulates silently as latent management debt. Third, the Driver Replacement Inflection Point is specified with four measurable early-warning indicators, including the condition where the marginal cost of talent acquisition exceeds the marginal revenue from innovation rents. Fourth, Micro-Covenants are introduced as minimal relational commitments planted during high-growth phases that create path dependence in the relational domain, and Covenant Governance is presented as the institutional archetype best suited to the post-dividend stage. The paper also theorizes the condition of relational lockout, in which a sustained history of extraction and broken promises becomes irreversible. Fifth, artificial intelligence is theorized as a friction catalyst: by compressing structural friction, AI accelerates firms' arrival at the Driver Replacement Inflection Point and renders relational friction more salient, making Covenant Governance an earlier and more urgent strategic investment. Together these arguments yield ten formal propositions, boundary conditions including sectoral velocity and capital intensity, and a construct-to-measurement map leveraging AI-enabled text analysis of employee reviews. DDRT respecifies when chaos is vital and when it is deadly, and recasts covenant governance as a strategic rather than merely ethical response to the exhaustion of external dividends.
